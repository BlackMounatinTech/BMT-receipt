Hiring guide · Michael’s call outline

Closer interview.

Read from top to bottom. Ask the main questions, follow up where useful, then run a short role-play. Allow about 20–30 minutes.

01 · Set the scene

Explain the role.

“Thanks for making time. I’m Michael, the founder of Black Mountain Technologies. I also run a contracting company. We help contractors improve their websites and social media so potential customers can see their work, understand their business and request a quote.

I handle outreach and book the meetings. I’m looking for a closer to run those calls, qualify the business, recommend the right package and follow up. I’ll ask about your experience, then we’ll do a short role-play.”

Our one-time packages, in USD

$1,500: 15 original posts published to both Facebook and Instagram, profile cleanup and a tailored 90-day content and online presence strategy.

$3,000: Everything above, plus a three-page website.

$4,500: 30 original posts published to both platforms, profile cleanup, a tailored 120-day strategy and a five-page website.

Optional ongoing: $2,000/month for 30 original posts published daily to both platforms.

Clients supply project photos and details. No guaranteed leads or jobs. Comment replies, inbox management and paid ads are outside these packages. Confirm exact website features and delivery dates before promising them.

02 · Their experience

What have they actually sold?

  1. “What have you personally sold, at what price, and who was buying it?”

    How many years have you been doing this? Have you sold marketing services or worked with contractors or small business owners?

  2. “What part of the sale did you handle yourself?”

    Were you qualifying, presenting, asking for payment and following up, or handling only one part?

  3. “Walk me through your actual results in your most recent role.”

    Over what period? How many calls were booked, how many attended and how many customers actually paid? What was the typical sale value?

  4. “How were those leads coming to you?”

    Ads, setters, cold outreach or existing customers? How warm were they? What did they already know, and had they heard the price?

  5. “Can you share a reference or a call recording you have permission to share?”

Listen for specific examples and numbers. A strong closing percentage means less if every lead was already ready to buy.

03 · Their sales process

How do they qualify and close?

  1. “Walk me through a typical call, from the opening to asking for the sale.”
  2. “How have you qualified leads before recommending a service?”

    How do you establish their need, budget, decision maker and timing? For our service, how would you check project photos and readiness to provide access?

  3. “Someone joins because a setter booked them, but they’re cold and barely know what we do. What do you do?”

    What if they say they don’t have a problem or only agreed to hear us out?

  4. “Tell me about someone you decided should not buy. Why?”
  5. “Their website is already good. How would you decide whether our social-only package is useful for them?”
04 · Objections and follow-up

Ask them to demonstrate.

  1. “I say, ‘That’s too expensive.’ What would you say next?”
  2. “I say, ‘We already get all our work through referrals.’ How do you respond?”
  3. “I say, ‘I need to think about it,’ or ‘I need to speak with my partner.’ What happens next?”
  4. “I ask, ‘How many jobs will this get me?’ What do you tell me?”
  5. “Tell me about a real objection you handled. What did you ask, and how did the conversation end?”
  6. “If they don’t buy on the call, walk me through your follow-up.”

    What do you send? When do you contact them again? How do you track it? When do you stop? Give an example of a sale won through follow-up.

Look for calm questions, direct answers and clear next steps. Watch for pressure, invented guarantees or immediate discounts.

05 · Working together

Can they do the job consistently?

  1. “What days and hours can you reliably take calls, and in which time zone?”

    Other clients or commitments? Earliest start date? Availability for follow-ups between meetings?

  2. “How do you keep a CRM updated and communicate after a call?”
  3. “If the leads aren’t qualified, how would you raise that with me and the setter?”
  4. “Tell me about feedback you received and how you applied it.”
  5. “What are you looking for long term? If this works well, would you want to grow with BMT and take on more responsibility?”

Before they start, agree commission, which tiers it applies to, payout timing, attribution and refund terms in writing. These are not established by this interview guide.

06 · Five-minute role-play

Let them lead the call.

“Let’s do a quick role-play. I own a concrete company. Michael booked this meeting with me. I’ve got good project photos, inconsistent social media and no website. I’m curious, but I haven’t decided I need anything. You take it from here.”

Give them a minute to review the packages. Answer as the contractor. Introduce one or two of these naturally:

  • “We already get work through referrals.”
  • “How do I know this will get me more jobs?”
  • “Three thousand dollars is a lot.”
  • “Just email me something.”

Notice whether they listen, qualify, recommend the right package, handle concerns honestly and ask for a decision or clear next step.

Afterward: “What do you think went well, and what would you change?” Give one piece of feedback and ask them to replay that part briefly.

07 · Their questions

Give them room to ask.

“Before we wrap up, what questions do you have for me about the offer, the leads or how we’d work together?”

08 · Close the interview

Explain what happens next.

“That sounds great. I appreciate your time. I’m going to send you a quick questionnaire so I have your experience and availability in writing. There’s a section at the bottom for any other questions you have for me.

Once you send it back, I’ll review your answers, respond to your questions and let you know whether I think we’re a good fit to move forward. I’m looking to move quickly, so I’ll aim to get back to you within six hours of receiving it today.”

Only use the six-hour commitment when you can meet it. Otherwise give a specific realistic time.

Open the follow-up questionnaire

Open the questionnaire, copy its text into a message or email and send it to the candidate. They reply with their answers. This page does not send messages or collect responses.
